One simulated season (seed 0, the same one the dashboard replays), traced through every stage of the loop: **photo → on-device detection → offline sync → aggregation → outbreak detection → alert → response**. Generated by `python3 scripts/walkthrough.py`; everything below is real pipeline output on simulated data.

## 1. One photo, on the phone

On **day 36 04:00** the farmer at **Farm 104 (Mukuyu)** photographs a coffee leaf. (Ground truth, unknown to anyone: the leaf has early rust lesions; 6% of leaves on this farm are affected.)

The on-device model runs offline and returns probabilities:

| Class | Probability |
|---|---|
| Healthy | 26% |
| Coffee leaf rust ← top | 50% |
| Leaf miner | 12% |
| Brown eye spot (Cercospora) | 11% |
| Phoma leaf spot | 1% |

The app shows *possible leaf rust, 50%*. On its own this is weak evidence; nobody would act on one uncertain photo. The app queues this payload (the photo itself is never uploaded):

```json
{"id": "obs-4322", "farm": 103, "captured_at": "day 36 04:00", "probs": [0.257, 0.498, 0.125, 0.106, 0.014], "model": "pestwatch-mnv3s-int8"}
```
141 bytes. The farm has no signal: the report syncs at **day 36 11:00**, 7 h later. The server uses the *capture* time for detection, and can only see the report once it has synced.

## 2. Background noise before the outbreak

In the 10 days before any pest arrives, farmers send 1,195 photos (120/day). 46 of them (3.8%) are flagged as leaf rust with ≥50% confidence, all false flags (mostly Cercospora / leaf miner look-alikes). The strongest cluster score in this period is **2.20** against an alert threshold of **3.21**: no alarm.

- **Extension training day: photo volume ×3** in Lutonyi, day 18 21:00–day 21 21:00: 27 photos/day there vs 16/day the week before. Strongest cluster centred there: 0.00 → no alarm.
- **Extension training day: photo volume ×3** in Chebukwa, day 32 23:00–day 35 23:00: 57 photos/day there vs 21/day the week before. Strongest cluster centred there: 0.00 → no alarm.
- **Look-alike flare-up (Cercospora / leaf miner): damage ×2.5** in Lutonyi, day 48 24:00–day 58 24:00: 19 photos/day there vs 9/day the week before. Strongest cluster centred there: 3.53 → alert, but 7 farms there really were infested by then.

Expected counts scale with the photos actually taken and with each farm's own history, so extra photos or a persistently noisy village don't look like an outbreak.

## 3. The outbreak starts (ground truth)

On **day 10 00:00**, after the rains, coffee leaf rust spores carried by wind and rain splash arrive near Mukuyu and infect **5 farms** over 3 days. The first lesions cover ~2% of leaves, far below the ~25% at which farmers notice rust unaided. It is 23 °C, close to rust's optimum, and the wind blows towards 286° at 2.6 m/s, so spores drift that way.

## 5. Outbreak detected: fusing the evidence

At **day 29 06:00** (19.2 days after the rust arrived), for a 2.2 km zone around Farm 106 (Mukuyu) (21 farms), the fusion model combines:

| Evidence | Value | Weight | Contribution (log-odds) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photo space-time scan (LLR) | 3.05 | +0.46 | +1.40 |
| Farmer confirmations (72 h) | 2 | +2.87 × log(1+n) | +3.15 |
| Baseline |  |  | -1.42 |

→ **P(outbreak) = 96%**, above the alert threshold 95% (calibrated so at most 10% of outbreak-free seasons ever alert). The alert is **report-based**. Forecast: at current temperatures (24 °C) damage would become visible in about **14 days**.

At this moment, 20 farms are infested but only **0** show damage a farmer would notice.

## 7. Farmers reply, in whatever language they write

Replies arrive by SMS, in the app, or as keypad presses on the voice call. A keyword grammar built from the catalogs works offline. It detects the reply's language, handles negation, accents and typos, and answers in the language the farmer used. A confirmed sighting becomes strong evidence (P(rust) = 0.95) for the scan; "none found" counts as a healthy report. Both also become labels for retraining the vision model.

## 8. Response and outcome

75% of alerted farmers inspect within 1–4 days (by tier). Inspection finds early lesions on infected farms, and control (fungicide and pruning, on extension advice) is applied. Over the season 102 farms were alerted; 56 farms were treated *before* damage became visible.

Same season, same random draws, with and without PestWatch:

|  | With PestWatch | Without |
|---|---|---|
| First warning | day 29 06:00 | – |
| First farmer notices damage | – | day 35 18:00 |
| Extension learns of outbreak (3 farmers + 5-day lag) | – | day 43 00:00 |
| Widespread (10% of farms visibly damaged) | – | day 63 12:00 |
| Farms ever infested | 76 | 126 |
| Farms visibly damaged at season end | 1 | 24 |
| Yield loss | $13,701 | $50,795 |
| Treatment cost | $2,875 | $2,318 |

**Lead time:** 13.8 days ahead of the conventional route, 34.2 days before widespread infestation. **Recall:** 74% of infested farms warned before visible damage. **Precision:** 83% of HIGH-alerted farms infested within 14 days (75% across all tiers). **Crop loss avoided:** $36,537 (73% of yield loss) for this region-season.

**Keeping farmers' trust (alert fatigue).** Each farmer received 1.7 alerts on average over the season (max 5); 73 lower-priority alerts were held back by the budget of 3 per fortnight (HIGH escalations always go through). Alert zones are stretched downwind and shortened upwind, with the same area as a circle. When an area stays quiet for 7 days, farmers get an all-clear (131 sent):

> (Kiswahili, app) Hali ni shwari: hakuna ripoti mpya za kutu ya kahawa karibu nawe kwa siku 7. Endelea kukagua miti yako mara moja kwa wiki.

**For extension services and agro-dealers.** At the first alert the HIGH zone covers 5 farms / 6 ha, so about **12 L** of copper fungicide (an assumed dose) should be pre-positioned at the nearest agro-dealer or cooperative.
 At day 29 12:00 the fused probability reached 95% with ≥5 farms reporting, which fires the **anticipatory-finance trigger**: 47 farms / 74 ha at risk → $1,115 released for control inputs (at $15/ha, an assumed rate).

**Cost.** Running PestWatch here this season costs about **$125** ($0.82 per farm): messaging $5, platform $46, lead-farmer stipends $60, extension visits $15. Against $36,537 of yield loss avoided that is a **benefit-cost ratio of 291×** (unit costs are assumptions in `CostConfig`).

## 9. The same loop, live over HTTP

`server.py` freezes this season at **day 52.5**, one step before the first alert, and accepts reports from the field app. This section was produced by actually calling it.

`GET /api/status?farm=80` → **WATCH** near Mukuyu: P(outbreak) 94.6% (alert at 95.1%; scan score 9.31), 15 reports from 7 farms; no alert for Farm 81 (Mukuyu).

The farmer at Farm 81 (Mukuyu) photographs a leaf with faint spots (model: *possible* leaf rust, 50%). After the phone regains signal it posts:

```
POST /api/observations  {"observations": [{"id": "walkthrough-1", "farm": 80, "captured_at": "now", "model": "demo-stub-v0", "probs": [0.28, 0.5, 0.04, 0.15, 0.03]}]}
```
Response: cluster is now **ALERT**, P(outbreak) 95.5% (was 94.6%). 
The response carries this farmer's alert (HIGH), rendered for their profile (en, app):

> Coffee leaf rust has been reported in your area. 16 reports from 8 farms in the last 72 hours. Risk: HIGH. Check your coffee within 24 hours. Check the underside of older leaves for orange, powdery spots. One report is from your farm. After checking, reply 1 if you found rust or 2 if none.

After inspecting, the farmer replies by SMS in Swahili, although their profile is en:

```
POST /api/inbound  {"farm": 80, "text": "Nimeona kutu kwenye majani ya kahawa", "channel": "sms"}
```
→ understood as **PEST_FOUND** (detected `sw`, matched kutu, nimeiona, nimeona); reply in sw: *Asante. Ripoti yako inawalinda majirani zako.
